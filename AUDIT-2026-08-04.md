## 1. What got solved since 2026-08-02

### 1.1 Two revenue bugs closed — both real, both verified fixed

**`credits` and `extend` granted permanent paid access.** Closed by
[db/migrations/0042_fulfillments.sql](db/migrations/0042_fulfillments.sql).

The mechanism: `fulfill()` used the 0020 unique index on
`subscriptions.stripe_session_id` as its idempotency guard, which forced it to write a
`subscriptions` row for *every* plan — including add-ons. Add-ons have no `grants.days`,
so `expires_at` came out NULL, and NULL is read as **open-ended** everywhere. A $4.99
credit pack therefore bought permanent access worth $24.99.

The fix separates the two jobs: `fulfillments` records "this Stripe session was
processed", `subscriptions` goes back to meaning "this user holds a pass". Idempotency
still lives in a unique index, still atomic under concurrent webhook retries.

**Verified in prod:**
- `fulfillments` exists, 8 rows — the backfill ran.
- `subscriptions` is down 8 → 7 rows; the bad `credits` row was deleted.
- **Zero rows with `expires_at IS NULL`** across all four plan keys. The bug class is gone,
  not just patched going forward.
- All sessions are `cs_test_*`; **zero `cs_live_*`**. No paying customer was ever affected.

**Business plans charged for nothing.** Closed by
[db/migrations/0041_business_waitlist.sql](db/migrations/0041_business_waitlist.sql) plus
the [landing/src/lib/plans.js](landing/src/lib/plans.js) rewrite.

`business_basic/featured/partner` declare tiers `'basic'`, `'featured'`, `'partner'` —
and **none of those keys exist in `FEATURES`**. So `bestTier()` resolved a paying business
subscriber all the way down to `free`. They'd have paid $19–$149/month and received the
free tier. Now the pricing page collects an email instead of a card.

`POST /api/business-waitlist` is live in [backend/server.js](backend/server.js) with a
5-req/min rate limit. `business_waitlist` exists in prod, 0 rows (nothing shipped yet).

### 1.2 Day Trip grant/copy mismatch fixed

`day_trip.grants.days` was `2` while every customer-facing surface said 24 hours. The
grant is what actually expires the pass, so it was the wrong number. Now `days: 1`.

consequence is bookkeeping, not outage. Correcting this because it changes priority —

## 6. Assessment

The two bugs fixed this week were the kind that silently destroy unit economics — a
$4.99 add-on granting $24.99 of access, and a $149/mo plan delivering the free tier.
Both were caught before a single live-mode charge. The `0042` header comment reasons
correctly about *why* conflating idempotency with entitlement was the root cause rather
than just patching the symptom.

Pulling the business ladder rather than shipping it half-built was the right call.

The gap now is not code quality — it's that **nothing is deployed and nothing has ever
processed a live payment.** The payment engine is more correct than most shipped
products, and it has never run in anger. Phase 0 is the whole job.

One structural risk to keep visible: the migration tracker drifted *again* with
0041/0042, which means whatever applies migrations isn't recording them. In a
single-destination world that's untidy. In the N-destination world PLATFORM.md
describes, it's the difference between "one command builds a destination" and "nobody
knows what's in destination #4's database." Fix the process, not just the rows.
